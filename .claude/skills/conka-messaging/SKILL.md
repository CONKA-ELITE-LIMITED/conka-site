# CONKA Messaging & Tone of Voice

Every piece of CONKA marketing copy follows this system. Read it fully before writing, then run the checklist at the end before handing anything over.

## 1. The core principle

**Educate the problem, never the ingredients.**

CONKA does not wait for people to understand nootropics. We make a specific, everyday moment feel uncomfortable, then make CONKA the obvious fix. Water2 is the model: they never explained filtration chemistry, they made tap water feel wrong. People don't care about ingredients. They care about how their day changes.

So copy should almost never lead with ingredients, mechanisms or "what's inside". Lead with the moment, the villain and the outcome.

## 2. The messaging system

| Layer | Flow | Clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hero line** | Flow first. | Clear before it counts. |
| **Promise (short)** | Get ahead. | Stay ahead. |
| **Promise (expanded)** | Calmness creates focus. Start the day with composure and control. | Clarity creates confidence. Clear is fast-acting, so you're sharp on demand. |
| **Momentum story** | Flow builds it. | Clear keeps it. |
| **The moment (rule)** | Shot before screen | The 5 minutes before |
| **The villain** | The inbox: the first thing you open decides whose agenda your day runs on | The back-to-back calendar: your head's still in the last thing |
| **Role** | The daily foundation | The on-demand tool |
| **Where it lives** | The fridge, every morning | Your bag or desk drawer |

**Both shots are taken in advance of effort. Neither is a fix after the fact.** Never position either shot as recovery, a pick-me-up after crashing, or a cure for tiredness.

- **Flow** is taken at the start of the working day, before opening the laptop or inbox. People are already up and have had their coffee. Not "the second you wake up", and not pre-sunrise.
- **Clear** is taken in the five minutes before anything that matters: the pitch, the big meeting, the deadline push, the exam, the start line. Its lead creative moment is the "starts in 5 minutes" calendar reminder.

**Analogy (use sparingly):** Flow is the race-morning breakfast; Clear is the gel before the climb.

### How the lines stack

Use the hierarchy, don't mix the layers up:

1. **Brand promise:** Get ahead. Stay ahead.
2. **System line:** Flow first. Clear before it counts.
3. **Ritual explainers:** Shot before screen. The 5 minutes before.

When there's room for one line only, use the system line: **Flow first. Clear before it counts.**

## 3. Audience

Lead with **productivity**: ambitious, proactive people with a lot on their plate. Examples: the finance professional, the founder, the Clapham dad, the 20-something corporate creator training for a marathon.

**Overwhelm is the unifying thread.** Different audiences describe the same thing differently:

- The high performer: too much on my plate, can't push as hard as I want.
- Younger audiences: can't get started, scattered, always on.
- Older audiences: not as sharp as I used to be, losing my stride.

Write about the **experience** so each group recognises itself. Never label or diagnose anyone. Ageing is a retention story for existing customers, not an acquisition angle.

## 4. Tone of voice

**CONKA sounds like:** a competitor who's already ahead and is letting you in on the edge. Confident, direct, ambitious, a little sporty. Never hype-y, never preachy, never clinical.

- **Short sentences. Active voice.** "You walk in sharp." Not "your mind is sharpened."
- **Talk to "you".** Second person, present tense.
- **Performance and sport language.** Start line, stride, momentum, the field, race day, edge, ahead. Ironman and endurance references fit the brand.
- **Social proof over fear.** "The rest of the field is already on it" beats "you're falling behind."
- **Club energy.** CONKA is a joker card in your back pocket. People should want the affiliation because it stands for being proactive, ambitious, productive and competitive.
- **Plain English over jargon.** No "optimise your cognitive performance".
- **British English spelling.** Optimise, colour, programme.
- **One idea per piece.** Pick one moment and one villain. Don't stack benefits.

**Words we use:** ahead, first, momentum, stride, composure, control, clarity, confidence, sharp, ready, at your best, when it counts, on your terms, the moment, the field, edge.

**Words we avoid:** biohack, hack, supercharge, unlock your potential, brain boost, limitless, 10x, miracle, cure, fix, crash, detox, "science-backed" as a standalone claim.

## 5. Structure templates

### Ads (all bottom of funnel, landing on the trial pack)

> **Villain or education → Rule → Promise → Offer**

- **Villain (first 3 seconds):** make the moment feel uncomfortable. The product doesn't appear yet.
- **Rule:** when to take the shot. Get it on screen before the offer appears.
- **Promise:** what they get. Get ahead / Stay ahead.
- **Offer:** the trial pack.

**Flow example**
> The first thing you open decides whose day it is.
> Open your inbox first and your morning is already running on someone else's agenda. Research by Gloria Mark at UC Irvine found our attention on a screen now shifts every 47 seconds on average. Start reactive, and you spend the whole day catching up.
> The people who stay ahead start differently.
> **Flow first. Shot before screen.** Get ahead, and start your day on your terms. Try the trial pack.

**Clear example**
> Your head's still in the last meeting.
> When you switch straight from one task to the next, part of your attention stays behind. Researcher Sophie Leroy calls it "attention residue." Most people walk into the moments that count still half in the last one.
> **Clear before it counts. The 5 minutes before.** Stay ahead, and walk in at your best. Try the trial pack.

**Statics**
> Your attention on a screen shifts every 47 seconds. Don't hand it to your inbox first. Flow first.
> Part of your attention stays in the last meeting. Walk into the next one clear. Clear before it counts.

### Organic social posts

Open with the moment or the villain, land one idea, close with the system line or a ritual explainer. Captions stay short: 1 to 3 short paragraphs. Hashtags minimal.

### Visual cues to reference in scripts and briefs

- **Flow:** the fridge opens, the shot is taken, the laptop opens. Repeat this story every time.
- **Clear:** the "starts in 5 minutes" notification with a shot beside it. Also the start line, the exam hall door, the moment before walking into the room.

Vary the person, setting and hook. Never vary the moment. Consistency builds the mental link.

### Landing pages

One page, one productivity promise. A "Sound familiar?" section lists everyday overwhelm moments in the audience's own words, so each group recognises itself without anyone being labelled.

## 6. Guardrails (non-negotiable)

CONKA sells food supplements in the UK, so copy must stay within ASA/CAP rules and the GB nutrition and health claims register.

- **Experience, not biology.** Describe how the customer feels ("composed", "sharp", "ready"), never what happens in the body. No cortisol, stress hormones, blood flow, neurotransmitters, brain chemistry or "boosts brain function".
- **Never name or imply a condition.** No ADHD, anxiety, depression, dementia, menopause, brain fog as a condition, COVID or health scares. Never suggest CONKA replaces or reduces medication.
- **No fatigue or recovery claims** unless they match an authorised claim word for word. Avoid "beats the slump", "cures tiredness", "sustained energy".
- **Research describes the problem, never the product.** None of the cited studies tested CONKA. Never imply they show CONKA works. Name the researcher rather than "studies show". Flag figures for verification before publishing.
- **"Fast-acting"** is the one factual product claim in the system and must be substantiated.
- **No superiority claims** ("the best", "the closest thing you can get to") without evidence.
- **Mechanism lives on the product page only**, using exact authorised claim wording for specific nutrients.

If a request pushes against a guardrail, write the compliant version and briefly explain the change.

## 7. Pre-publish checklist

Before handing over copy, check:

1. Does it lead with a moment or villain, not ingredients?
2. Is there only one moment and one idea?
3. Is each shot positioned before effort, never as a fix after it?
4. Are Flow and Clear in the right moments (start of work day / 5 minutes before)?
5. Does it use the approved lines exactly as written?
6. Is every benefit an experience, not a bodily effect?
7. Is no condition named or implied, and no medication mentioned?
8. Are research figures attributed and flagged for verification?
9. Short sentences, active voice, British spelling?
10. Would an ambitious, competitive person want to be part of this?
